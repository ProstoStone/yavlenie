A glossary of Model of the Phenomenon
Brief explanations of phenomenon, one, distinction, concept, and experience.
Reference
2026-11-03

This glossary explains how terms are used in *Model of the Phenomenon*. It isn't a universal philosophical dictionary. The model's specific meanings don't automatically apply to every sentence of *Monophenomenalism*.

The English originals distinguish **experiencing** (переживание) from **experience** (опыт). **Sensory experiencing** translates чувственное переживание. These distinctions are preserved here.

## Phenomenon

In the model, that which is and exists as sensory experiencing. The phenomenon isn't placed behind experiencing as a hidden thing that produces it. In the exposition of monophenomenalism, the word denotes everything that exists within the adopted position.

## One

Not one object among others, and not the identity of all things. The word points to not introducing a separate second foundation. Differences among colors, pain, speech, people, and events remain. Unity doesn't mean every experiencing is accessible to a single observer.

## Sensory experiencing

The term encompasses sensations, emotions, thoughts, and experienced states. It isn't limited to the data of particular sense organs and doesn't require an owner of experiencing to be established beforehand.

## Distinction

The way experiencing is as distinguished. Not an operation performed by a separate agent on initially empty material. Green is distinguished by its greenness; a word doesn't create its quality.

## Concept

A system of distinctions. In this terminology, a concept isn't necessarily a word, a conscious thought, or a theory. Calling pain a conceptual distinction doesn't reduce it to a thought about pain. Its particular content needs to be preserved in each examination.

## Structuring

How concepts exist as experience. “Structure” doesn't denote causal production or the processing of raw material over time. The fourth and fifth theses are considered together.

## Experience

The phenomenon structured through concepts. Not a separate product or an independent subject's property. The order in which terms are explained isn't the order in which experience comes into existence.

## The “I”

In the model, a distinction through which experience is described as “mine,” “not mine,” or “happening to me.” Not introducing a separate owner doesn't abolish people, their particularities, relationships, or practical responsibility.

## A property and its negation

The model doesn't attribute an independent nature behind distinctions to the phenomenon. “There is no time” shouldn't be turned into a new property of a hidden thing: timelessness. Temporal and causal descriptions remain within particular experience.

## Consistency

Context needs clarification: logical consistency, agreement with observations, and experienced harmony don't automatically become one criterion. A consistent description doesn't by itself prove the starting philosophical position.

## Self-application

The model, reading it, doubt, and criticism are also considered within experience. Being able to describe an objection in the model's terms doesn't make the objection false or prove all the theses true.
